# Customer discovery plan: five Prague interviews

FR-3. Role: pm. Date drafted: 2026-09-14.

## Purpose

Answer BRIEF.md's Q1–Q4 with real people who make call sheets, not
assumptions. This plan is the instrument; running it (recruiting real
candidates and sending invitations) is out of this routine's reach — see
"Sourcing candidates" and the INBOX item below.

For conversations about sudden plan changes, use
`plan-change-interviews.md` (FR-208) instead of the script below: this one
cannot separate deciding from telling.

## Target roles

The person who *drafts* call-sheet times/locations, the person who
*approves* them, and the person who *distributes* them may be three
different people (BRIEF.md Q1). Recruit across these roles so the
interview set can actually surface that split rather than assume one role
does all three:

- **1st AD** — most likely to draft and approve on mid/large productions.
- **2nd AD** — most likely to draft and distribute; the hands-on owner on
  many shoots.
- **Production coordinator** — most likely on productions without a
  standing 2nd AD, and often the person actually clicking "send".

Target five interviews total, coded by production size at recruitment
time (self-reported, confirmed in the interview's first minute):

| Bucket | Definition |
|---|---|
| Short | fewer than 15 crew |
| Feature (indie/mid) | fewer than 50 crew |
| Feature/TV (large) | 50+ crew |

Aim for at least one interview outside the short-film bucket — the
incumbents in FR-2's teardown (StudioBinder, Movie Magic, Croogloo) sell
to production offices running larger shoots, so an all-short-film sample
would under-test Q4 and Q6.

## Sourcing candidates (and the failure case)

This routine has no access to the founder's personal network and cannot
name real candidates or send email to them (`TEAM.md`'s approval contract:
email only to `sandovabarb@gmail.com`). Recruiting is therefore an action
for the founder, seeded by this plan:

1. **Founder's network first.** Direct asks to ADs/coordinators the
   founder has worked with or can reach through one warm intro are fastest
   and highest-response.
2. **If fewer than five say yes from that network**, don't shrink the
   sample — widen the channel instead (BRIEF.md's own failure case for
   this item):
   - **FAMU** (Film and TV School of the Academy of Performing Arts,
     Prague) — production students and recent graduates who 1st/2nd AD or
     coordinate on school and thesis productions; FAMU's own production
     office or student production boards are the way in.
   - **Czech Film Commission** production listings — productions currently
     shooting or prepping in the Czech Republic; find crew contacts
     through the listed production company.
   - **Public filming-permit data** (Prague Film Office / municipal permit
     records, where published) — a secondary way to identify active
     productions and their production office contact.
3. Whichever channel a candidate comes from, code it in the note-taking
   template (below) so the interview set's sourcing bias is visible, not
   hidden, when BRIEF.md Q1–Q6 conclusions get drawn from it.

## The 30-minute script

Each question below maps directly to a BRIEF.md open question and its
"how we answer it" method — the script exists so the interview produces
exactly what that method needs, not a general conversation.

**0. Consent and framing (2 min)**
"Thanks for making time. This is early customer discovery for a call-sheet
tool — I want to understand how you actually work today, not pitch you
anything. Okay if I take notes? Nothing you say will be attributed by name
without asking you first."
Confirm: production size bucket (short / indie-feature / large
feature-TV) and the interviewee's role.

**1. Who does what (Q1) — 6 min**
Ask separately, in this order, so the interviewee doesn't collapse three
roles into one answer:
- "On your last production, who actually typed up the call sheet — times,
  locations, the works?"
- "Who signed off on it before it went out?"
- "Who hit send / handed it out?"
Follow-up: "Was that the same person every time, or did it depend on the
day/department?"

**2. Current tool and cost in time (Q2) — 8 min**
- "Walk me through it: the schedule is locked for tomorrow — what
  happens, step by step, until everyone on the crew has tomorrow's call
  sheet in hand?"
- "What tool or workflow is that — email, a PDF, WhatsApp, StudioBinder,
  something else? Name whatever you actually used, not what you wish
  you'd used."
- "Roughly how long does that whole process take, from schedule-locked to
  everyone having it?"
- If a same-day revision came up: "What happened then — did the process
  repeat from scratch?"

**3. What goes wrong (Q3) — 6 min**
- Unprompted first: "What's gone wrong with call sheets on a shoot you've
  worked — the actual failures, not hypotheticals?" Let them name up to 3
  before prompting.
- Then read the candidate list and ask them to confirm/add/rank top 3:
  late revisions, wrong call times, no-signal locations, a crew member
  missing an update, printing cost/waste.

**4. Who pays (Q4) — 6 min**
- "Who owns the budget line for whatever software or process you use for
  call sheets — the production company, a line item you control, or does
  it come out of your own pocket?"
- Price ladder, read in order, stop at the first "no":
  "Could you expense this without a second approval if it were... free?
  ...low tens of dollars a month? ...roughly what [name the incumbent
  they mentioned in Q2, with its FR-2 price] costs?"
- "What's the highest of those you could say yes to without asking
  someone else first?"

**5. Switching (Q6, bonus if time allows) — 2 min**
"What would have to be true for you to switch tools mid-production?"

**Close (2 min)**
Thank them; ask if they know one or two other people (any of the three
roles) worth talking to — this is also how the sample grows past the
founder's own network.

## What happens after

Interview notes feed BRIEF.md's Q1–Q6 directly and its kill criteria:
fewer than 2 of 5 interviewees naming call-sheet distribution in their
unprompted-or-confirmed top-3 (Q3) means stop and re-frame the wedge; no
price-ladder tier any interviewee can expense without a second approval
(Q4) means there is no plausible payer. Whoever runs the interviews should
re-read those two thresholds in BRIEF.md before declaring FR-3 answered.
