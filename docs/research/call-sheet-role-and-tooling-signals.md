FR-22. Role: pm. Date checked: 2026-09-21. Serves BRIEF.md Q1 (who drafts,
approves, distributes the call sheet) and Q2 (what tool/workflow a
production names today) — the desk-research half only. FR-3's interview
script (`docs/research/discovery-plan.md`) asks these same questions
directly of real people and remains blocked on the founder recruiting

`WebFetch` is blocked by this session's egress proxy for every domain

## Who assembles, approves, and distributes: role signals found

- **A real, named Prague production coordinator confirms call sheets are
  in scope for the role.** Kristyna Chalupova's LinkedIn profile lists
  herself as a "Freelance Production Coordinator & Production Manager"
  based in Prague, with named experience including "setting up shoots,
  crewing, contracting, call sheets and scheduling, hiring kit, managing
  and reconciling floats, expenses, and managing small production
  budgets," and names Stillking Films — a large Prague-based international
  service-production company — among her credits
  ([linkedin.com/in/kristyna-chalupova-296a2740](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kristyna-chalupova-296a2740/),
  checked 2026-09-21). This is the single strongest data point this shift
  found: a real Czech-market production coordinator naming call sheets as
  her own responsibility, not a job-description generality. **Coded by
  size**: Stillking Films services feature/TV-scale international
  productions (>50 crew), so this signal sits at the large end, not the
  short-film end.
- **A second Prague profile names the adjacent role without confirming
  the call sheet task specifically.** Zuzana Valkova's LinkedIn profile
  describes work as "Producer/Production Coordinator" on smaller Czech
  productions — short films, TV, and online commercials
  ([linkedin.com/in/zuzana-valkova-984773105](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zuzana-valkova-984773105/),
  checked 2026-09-21) — plausibly the short/feature-under-50 tier by the
  production types named, but the search snippet did not surface whether
  her listed skills include call sheets specifically; **unverified** for
  the tooling/task claim, only the role and size tier are confirmed.
- **Czech occupational-standard descriptions (NSP.cz) name coordination,
  not the call sheet document itself.** The national job-classification
  entry for "První asistent režie" (1st AD) describes work activities as
  coordinating and directing the crew's work during shooting per the
  director's needs, and conveying information from the director to other
  departments
  ([nsp.cz/jednotka-prace/prvni-asistent-rezie](https://nsp.cz/jednotka-prace/prvni-asistent-rezie),
  checked 2026-09-21, snippet only — the page itself is egress-blocked).
  This is consistent with the 1st AD owning schedule communication but
  does not name "rozpis natáčení" or "call sheet" as a specific
  deliverable; **unverified** as a direct confirmation, only as
  consistent context.
- **The generic "produkční" (production coordinator) description matches
  the international division of labour, not a Czech-specific one.**
  Prace.cz's and Jobs.cz's occupational-encyclopedia entries describe the
  produkční as handling budget planning, logistics, and communication
  across "authors, directors, set designers, artists, technicians, and
  partners," with the explicit framing "snaží se, aby se všichni sešli ve
  stejnou chvíli na stejném místě" (tries to make sure everyone arrives at
  the same place at the same time) — a plain-language description of what
  a call sheet exists to guarantee, without naming the document
  ([prace.cz/encyklopedie-profesi/p/produkcni](https://www.prace.cz/encyklopedie-profesi/p/produkcni/),
  [jobs.cz/poradna/profese/p/produkcni](https://www.jobs.cz/poradna/profese/p/produkcni/),
  checked 2026-09-21). Not size-coded; these are generic occupational
  descriptions, not production-specific accounts.
- **International sources (not Czech, cited for contrast only) split the
  task between 2nd AD and production coordinator.** SetHero's own 2nd AD
  job-description article states the 2nd AD "is responsible for creating
  call sheets," working closely with the production coordinator
  ([sethero.com/blog/2nd-assistant-director-the-ultimate-job-description-guide](https://sethero.com/blog/2nd-assistant-director-the-ultimate-job-description-guide/),
  checked 2026-09-21, cited also by FR-2's teardown for a different
  purpose). Wrapbook's and other US-market resume/CV guides describe
  production coordinators as the ones who "create and send out call
  sheets" and "issue call sheets and sides to cast and crew, tracking
  acknowledgements and last-minute changes" (generic CV-writing guidance,
  not Czech-specific, checked 2026-09-21). These match FR-3's own script
  framing (drafts/approves/distributes can be three different people) but
  are **not** Czech-market evidence — included only to show the
  international pattern this shift's one real Czech data point
  (Chalupova) is consistent with.

## What tool or workflow a production names today: tooling signals found

- **No Czech-specific tool naming surfaced.** Four targeted searches for
  a named Czech or Prague production using StudioBinder, Yamdu, Movie
  Magic, or any specific call-sheet tool returned no matching result —
  neither a job posting, a CV, nor a forum post named a specific tool in
  a Czech production context. This is a real gap in what `WebSearch` can
  surface, not evidence that Czech productions use no tool; marked
  **unverified**, per this item's failure case, the same convention FR-21
  used for its own unresolved forum searches.
- **Generic (non-Czech) CV keyword guidance names the tool landscape a
  production coordinator is expected to know**: Movie Magic
  Scheduling/Budgeting, ShotGrid, Avid, Resolve, Premiere, Celtx, Slack,
  and Excel are the tools most often listed as expected skills across
  English-language production-coordinator CV guides
  ([enhancv.com/resume-examples/production-coordinator](https://enhancv.com/resume-examples/production-coordinator/),
  checked 2026-09-21). This is US/UK hiring-market convention, not a
  Czech finding — cited only as the baseline FR-3 interviews should test
  a Czech answer against, exactly as this item's dod requires.
- **No Facebook group, Reddit thread, or other public forum discussion of
  Czech call-sheet workflow surfaced** despite two targeted searches for
  "filmový štáb" and WhatsApp/email/PDF distribution habits. **Unverified**
  whether informal workflow discussion exists there; the same class of
  gap FR-21 hit for switching-cost discussion.
- **FAMU's own "Production Regulations" page exists but could not be
  read.** `www.famu.cz/en/faculty/studio-famu/production-regulations/`
  surfaced as a real, on-topic URL in two separate searches, but
  `WebFetch` is not usable on it (egress-blocked, consistent with every
  other domain tried this shift) and `WebSearch`'s own snippet for it
  returned only page navigation with no body text, alongside an unrelated
  US film school's ("FSU Film Handbook," Florida A&M University —
  a false-positive name collision with FAMU, the Prague film school, not
  a Czech source) call-sheet distribution convention that is explicitly
  **not** cited here as a Czech finding, only noted to flag the
  collision for a future shift searching the same terms. FAMU's actual
  student-production call-sheet convention (relevant to the short/<15
  crew tier) stays **unverified**; a future shift with working `WebFetch`
  or a founder session should read the FAMU page directly.

point. It is not a substitute for FR-3's own interview questions — "who

expect to hear and gives one real name's worth of confirmation that the
call-sheet task does sit inside the Prague production-coordinator role,
at least at the large-production end.

## Failure case: no source names a role and a tool together

Per this item's dod, this is exactly what happened for tooling: every
role signal found (Chalupova's LinkedIn listing, the generic occupational
descriptions) names call-sheet responsibility, but none of them, nor any
separate search, named a specific tool a Czech production actually uses.
This document does not infer a link between the two — it states the gap
above rather than guessing that any Czech production uses StudioBinder,
Yamdu, or any other named tool.
